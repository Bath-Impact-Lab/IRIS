Abstract

This paper presents IRIS, a markerless motion-capture platform designed to provide accessible, real-time pose estimation using commodity hardware. The paper reviews existing motion-capture technologies, identifies gaps in the state of the art, and describes the principal innovations introduced by IRIS.

IRIS runs locally on a standard PC or laptop with low-cost webcams. It is designed for quick deployment: users can place the cameras and start capture, while the system handles multi-camera configuration automatically, avoiding manual wand or board calibration in field settings. In real time, ingest follows the native frame rate of the connected hardware; commodity webcams currently allow up to around 120 Hz, with higher frame rates possible where hardware allows. For offline workflows, IRIS can ingest at the native rate of the source footage.

IRIS is designed around three core pillars:

  • Privacy and Data Governance: Performs local processing on-device to comply with strict data-governance standards (e.g., GDPR) for sensitive healthcare and sports research.
  • Accessibility and Low Cost: Operates on standard desktop/laptop PCs using low-cost commodity webcams, eliminating the need for expensive dedicated hardware.
  • Speed and Accuracy: Ingests video at native hardware frame rates (up to 120 Hz with webcams, and higher where hardware permits) while maintaining low joint position error for reliable real-time and offline motion analysis.
